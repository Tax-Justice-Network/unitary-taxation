# Inventory of Publicly Available Datasets on Extractive Industry Values, Natural Resource Production, and Resource Revenues

*Compiled: 24 March 2026*
*Purpose: Identify data sources beyond EITI, WIDER, and ICTD that could help estimate how much royalties countries should receive for multinational extraction of their natural resources.*

---

## 1. World Bank -- Total Natural Resources Rents (% of GDP)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Provider** | World Bank, World Development Indicators (WDI) |
| **What it covers** | Total natural resources rents as a percentage of GDP. Sum of oil rents, natural gas rents, coal rents (hard and soft), mineral rents, and forest rents. Rents are estimated as the difference between the price of a commodity and the average cost of producing it (i.e., economic rent). |
| **URL** | https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.TOTL.RT.ZS |
| **Variables** | NY.GDP.TOTL.RT.ZS (total rents), plus sub-indicators: oil rents (NY.GDP.PETR.RT.ZS), natural gas rents (NY.GDP.NGAS.RT.ZS), coal rents (NY.GDP.COAL.RT.ZS), mineral rents (NY.GDP.MINR.RT.ZS), forest rents (NY.GDP.FRST.RT.ZS) |
| **Country coverage** | ~200 countries and territories |
| **Year coverage** | 1970--2021 (varies by country) |
| **Linkable to companies?** | No -- country-level aggregates only |
| **File format** | CSV, XML, Excel (via DataBank or direct download) |
| **Notes** | Based on methodology from "The Changing Wealth of Nations." Crucial for estimating the total value of resource extraction per country. CC BY-4.0 licence. |

| Field | Detail |

| **Data explorer** | https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/changing-wealth-of-nations/data |

| **Country coverage** | 151 countries |
| **Year coverage** | 1995--2020 |
| **Linkable to companies?** | No -- country-level aggregates |
| **File format** | Excel (.xlsx), accessible via WDI DataBank |
| **Notes** | Unique source for dollar-value estimates of natural capital stocks. Essential for understanding total resource wealth that a country possesses. |

## 3. World Bank -- Commodity Price Data ("The Pink Sheet")

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Provider** | World Bank, Prospects Group |
| **What it covers** | Monthly and annual price data for ~70 commodities in nominal and real terms, including energy (crude oil, natural gas, coal), metals (aluminium, copper, gold, iron ore, lead, nickel, tin, zinc, etc.), precious metals, and fertilizers. |
| **URL** | https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/18675f1d1639c7a34d463f59263ba0a2-0050012025/world-bank-commodities-price-data-the-pink-sheet |
| **Variables** | Monthly and annual prices for each commodity in nominal USD; real (constant USD) prices; price indices |
| **Country coverage** | Global benchmark prices (not country-specific) |
| **Year coverage** | 1960--present (monthly from ~1980) |
| **Linkable to companies?** | No -- market prices |
| **File format** | Excel (.xlsx): CMO-Historical-Data-Annual.xlsx, CMO-Historical-Data-Monthly.xlsx; also PDF monthly reports |
| **Notes** | Critical for valuing production volumes at market prices. Can be combined with USGS/BGS production volumes to estimate production values per country. |

## 5. IMF -- Fiscal Analysis of Resource Industries (FARI)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Provider** | International Monetary Fund, Fiscal Affairs Department |
| **What it covers** | A fiscal modelling framework (not a dataset per se) for analysing fiscal regimes applied to extractive industries. Discounted cash flow models set up to reflect tax accounting rules and government payments. Used for fiscal regime design, revenue forecasting, and revenue risk assessment. |
| **URL** | https://www.imf.org/en/topics/fiscal-policies/fiscal-analysis-of-resource-industries |
| **Methodology paper** | https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/tnm/2016/tnm1601.pdf |
| **Variables** | Model inputs: production profiles, commodity prices, capital/operating costs, fiscal terms (royalties, income tax, production sharing, etc.). Model outputs: government take, effective tax rates, NPV of government revenue. |
| **Country coverage** | Not a standing dataset -- applied per country/project |
| **Year coverage** | N/A (modelling tool) |
| **Linkable to companies?** | Can be applied at project level |
| **File format** | Excel-based model |
| **Notes** | Useful as a methodological reference for how to estimate what governments should receive. The 2021 updated models are publicly available in English, French, and Spanish. Related 2024 working paper: "Cash Flow Analysis of Fiscal Regimes for Extractive Industries." |

## 14. ResourceProjects.org (NRGI)

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Provider** | Natural Resource Governance Institute |
| **What it covers** | Payments made by extractive companies to governments worldwide, based on mandatory disclosure regulations (EU Accounting/Transparency Directives, UK, Canada, Norway). Project-level payment data. |
| **URL** | https://resourceprojects.org/ |

| **Country coverage** | Global (wherever EU/UK/Canada/Norway-listed companies operate) |
| **Year coverage** | 2014--present (updated weekly with new disclosures) |
| **Linkable to companies?** | Yes -- 900+ companies with project-level granularity |
| **File format** | ZIP download (CSV), web interface |
| **Notes** | HIGHLY RELEVANT. This is the most granular publicly available dataset linking companies, projects, countries, and specific payment types (including royalties). Covers companies listed in EU, UK, Canada, Norway. Does not cover US-listed companies (no equivalent US regulation). |

## ANALYTICAL APPROACH: How These Datasets Fit Together

To estimate how much royalties countries *should* receive:

1. **Establish production volumes**: USGS + BGS + EIA + Energy Institute data give physical production (tonnes, barrels, bcm) per country per commodity.

2. **Value production at market prices**: World Bank Pink Sheet commodity prices x production volumes = estimated gross production value per country.

3. **Determine applicable royalty/fiscal terms**: ResourceContracts.org shows what rates are written into contracts. IMF FARI provides modelling methodology. OECD/IMF data shows what effective tax rates are.

4. **Calculate expected royalty revenue**: Gross production value x contractual royalty rate = expected royalty revenue.

5. **Compare to actual revenue collected**: ResourceProjects.org (company-level payments), EITI data, NRGI revenue dataset, IMF WoRLD, and OECD Revenue Statistics show what was actually collected.

6. **Identify the gap**: Expected minus actual = royalty gap. World Bank rents data provides a cross-check on total rents.

7. **Context**: NRGI Resource Governance Index, IMF Article IV reports, and Publish What You Pay assessments help explain governance factors behind any gaps.
