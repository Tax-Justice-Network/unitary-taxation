==============================================================================
COUNTRY RESOURCE INFO  —  SAU  (Saudi Arabia)
==============================================================================

This sheet details how the profit and tax reported for this country in country-by-country reports (CbCR)
are adjusted to take out resource-sector (oil, gas and mining) activity, before the
remaining profit is reallocated between countries in the unitary-taxation analysis.
Resource-related profits that are currently taxed as part of the source country's
resource-rent capture strategy are removed from the profit pool to be distributed under
unitary taxation. Resource-related taxes are also removed from the paid taxes.
Otherwise, countries would lose the revenues they legitimately receive
from multinationals' extraction of their resources.

All monetary values are in billion US dollars.

RESOURCES
Relevant resources: oil & gas.

RESOURCE RENT CAPTURE REGIME
Overall, resource PROFIT in this country faces a combined effective tax rate of about 85%
(corporate income tax plus any resource surtax / petroleum tax — a rate on profit, NOT on the
economic rent or on gross revenue; source: Saudi Royal Decree A/152/2017 + IMF Article IV).
The government's take arrives in three forms (split source: actual realized split from manual_distributed payment streams), which affect the reported
profit and tax differently, so the correction tracks them separately:
  - Royalties / production entitlements, paid before profit is taxed: 0.25 of the take.
    These are a cost that already lowered the reported profit, so they are not deducted again
    from reported profit (the 'resources included' view instead adds them back).
  - Resource income tax / corporate tax, paid on profit: 0.2 of the take.
    This is the part that is removed from the reported tax line when resources are excluded.
  - State or national-oil-company dividends from government equity stakes: 0.55 of the take.
    This is a return on ownership rather than a tax.

HOW THE RESOURCE PROFIT IS REMOVED FOR THIS COUNTRY
This country is corrected by the ETR-gap method: the profit removed is inferred from the CbCR
tax itself — the tax paid above what the 20% statutory rate would imply, converted to profit
at the 20% resource rate. The royalty / income-tax / dividend split above feeds the
'resources included' and minimum-royalty views, and is the fallback in any year where the gap
is inert (observed ETR at or below the statutory rate).

TAX RATES
General statutory corporate income tax rate: 0.200. This is the rate applied to ordinary
(non-resource) company profit, and it is the baseline the correction compares against.
Source: the project's statutory CIT-rate dataset (OECD Corporate Tax Statistics and the Tax Foundation).
Resource profit-tax rate: 0.2. This is the rate at which resource-sector profit is
taxed, and it converts a known resource tax payment into the profit behind it (profit = tax / rate).
Source: resource_profit_tax_rate.csv (income-tax-based, per commodity).
This country's TOTAL government take from the resource sector is about 0.85 of profit, but
that figure bundles three flows together: royalties, income tax, and state dividends.
Only the income-tax part (0.2) is used to convert the CbCR income-tax line back
into profit; the royalties and dividends are removed separately (as pre-profit royalties and
state-equity income), so using the full 0.85 here would double-count them and understate
the resource profit.
Notes on the tax regime: Aramco-only effective rate post-2017 reform: 50% income tax + royalty + dividend; aggregate fiscal take ≈ 85%

ALLOCATION OF RESOURCE-RELATED PROFITS TO DIFFERENT HEADQUARTERS
The resource profit removed above is attributed to the home (headquarter) countries of the
multinationals that earned it, so that under unitary taxation it is taken out of the correct
parents' worldwide profit pools instead of being reallocated like ordinary profit.
For Saudi Arabia, only a national resource-revenue total is known, so it is split across headquarter
countries using Orbis ownership shares for each commodity, anchored on operator-level
payment-to-government data where it exists.
About 93.0% of the take is kept by this country's own state and domestic firms
(the domestic share); the take is attributed across headquarters as follows:
    SAU (domestic): 93.0%
    USA: 1.6%
    CHN: 0.8%
    CAN: 0.5%
    other (108 more headquarters): 4.1%
